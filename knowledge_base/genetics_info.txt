A splice junction is a critical boundary in a DNA sequence. It marks the point where coding regions (exons) and non-coding regions (introns) meet. 
The Exon-Intron (EI) junction is often called the "donor" site, and it frequently contains the nucleotide sequence 'GT'. 
The Intron-Exon (IE) junction is often called the "acceptor" site, and it frequently contains the nucleotide sequence 'AG'.
Identifying splice junctions is essential for understanding how genes are expressed and how genetic mutations can lead to diseases like cancer.
